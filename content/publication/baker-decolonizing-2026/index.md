summary: Big, open data approaches assume that data are an objective representation of a complex reality, and rely on fixed classificatory schemes to enable aggregation and rigid labels to facilitate machine-readability. Scholars have strongly challenged these assumptions and have demonstrated their impact on the quality of digital archaeological data and limitations in their reuse (Atici et al. 2013; Gupta 2020). More fundamentally, this Eurocentric view obscures systems of oppression and extraction, resulting in barriers and unequal benefits from digital technologies, and it diminishes the rights and interests of Indigenous Peoples and descendant communities to their heritage.
